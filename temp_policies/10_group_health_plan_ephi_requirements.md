**Policy Type**: HIPAA Security Policy (Specific to Group Health Plans)
**Frequency**: Yearly Review
**Department**: HR/Benefits
**Status**: ⚠️ NEEDS COMPLIANCE WRITER

## Coverage

This policy addresses requirements for the following HIPAA Security Rule controls:
- **Control 17**: Group Health Plan ePHI Safeguards

---

## Applicability

**⚠️ IMPORTANT APPLICABILITY NOTE:**

This policy applies ONLY to organizations that are Group Health Plans as defined by HIPAA. If {{COMPANY}} is NOT a Group Health Plan, this policy is NOT APPLICABLE and should be removed from the policy set.

### Group Health Plan Definition

A Group Health Plan is:
- An employee welfare benefit plan under ERISA that provides medical care
- Includes self-insured health plans
- Includes employer-sponsored health insurance plans with 50+ participants
- Does NOT include fully-insured small group plans (<50 participants)

### Common Scenarios

**This policy APPLIES if:**
- {{COMPANY}} sponsors a self-insured health plan
- {{COMPANY}} has a large group health plan (50+ participants) where the plan sponsor has access to ePHI

**This policy DOES NOT APPLY if:**
- {{COMPANY}} offers fully-insured health insurance (insurance carrier is the covered entity)
- {{COMPANY}} has a small group plan (<50 participants)
- {{COMPANY}} does not offer health benefits
- {{COMPANY}} is a healthcare provider or health plan (different HIPAA rules apply)

## Policy Statements

**[TO BE COMPLETED - See guidance below]**

### Required Sections:

#### 1. Plan Document Amendment Requirements

The Group Health Plan documents must be amended to incorporate provisions requiring the plan sponsor to:

- Implement administrative, physical, and technical safeguards to protect ePHI
- Ensure adequate separation between health plan and plan sponsor is supported by reasonable safeguards
- Ensure that adequate separation is supported by security measures
- Ensure that plan sponsor will not use or disclose ePHI for employment-related actions
- Report to the health plan any security incident involving ePHI
- Provide for return or destruction of ePHI upon plan termination

#### 2. Adequate Separation

**Separation of Functions:**
- Designate specific employees who may access ePHI (limited class of employees)
- Access limited to plan administration functions only
- No access to ePHI for employment-related decisions (hiring, promotion, discipline)
- Chinese Wall between HR benefits function and HR employment function

**Permitted Access for Plan Administration:**
- Plan enrollment and eligibility verification
- Claims processing and adjudication (if self-insured)
- COBRA administration
- HIPAA Privacy Official functions
- Compliance and audit activities
- Legal review of plan matters

**Prohibited Uses:**
- Performance evaluations
- Hiring or termination decisions
- Promotion or compensation decisions
- Workplace investigations or disciplinary actions
- General HR decision-making

#### 3. Plan Sponsor Safeguards

Plan sponsor must implement HIPAA Security Rule safeguards for ePHI received from the health plan:

- Administrative Safeguards: Policies, training, access controls, incident response
- Physical Safeguards: Secure storage, workstation security, device controls
- Technical Safeguards: Access controls, audit logs, encryption, authentication
- Documentation: Policies and procedures documented and retained for 6 years

#### 4. Certification Requirement

Plan sponsor must certify to the health plan that:
- Plan documents have been amended per HIPAA requirements
- Plan sponsor will safeguard ePHI appropriately
- Plan sponsor will report security incidents to the health plan
- Limited class of employees designated for ePHI access

#### 5. Security Incident Reporting

Plan sponsor must report security incidents to the health plan:
- Unauthorized access to plan ePHI
- ePHI breaches
- Timeline: Within 30 days of discovery (or as specified in plan documents)

#### 6. Third-Party Administrators (TPAs)

If using TPAs for plan administration:
- Execute Business Associate Agreement with TPA
- TPA must comply with HIPAA Security Rule
- Plan sponsor oversight of TPA security practices

## Procedures

**[TO BE COMPLETED]**

Should include detailed procedures for:

### Plan Document Amendment Process
1. Engage legal counsel specializing in ERISA and HIPAA
2. Draft amendment incorporating required provisions
3. Review with health plan or insurance carrier
4. Board of Directors or Plan Committee approval
5. Distribute amended plan documents to participants
6. Maintain copies per ERISA retention requirements

### Designating Limited Class of Employees
1. Identify positions requiring ePHI access for plan administration
2. Document job functions requiring ePHI
3. Obtain management approval
4. Provide HIPAA training to designated employees
5. Document list of designated employees
6. Update list annually or when changes occur

### Plan ePHI Access Request
1. Employee requests access to plan ePHI
2. Manager verifies business need (plan administration only)
3. Verify employee is designated in plan documents
4. HIPAA training completion verified
5. Access granted with audit logging
6. Access reviewed quarterly

### Security Incident Reporting (Plan Sponsor to Health Plan)
1. Security incident involving plan ePHI discovered
2. Incident investigation by Security Officer
3. Incident details documented
4. Report to health plan within 30 days
5. Cooperate with health plan breach determination
6. Implement corrective actions

## Plan Document Amendment Template Language

**[TO BE COMPLETED]**

Example language to be incorporated into Group Health Plan documents:

*"The Plan Sponsor agrees to:*
- *Implement administrative, physical, and technical safeguards that reasonably and appropriately protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of the electronic Protected Health Information that it creates, receives, maintains, or transmits on behalf of the Group Health Plan;*
- *Ensure that adequate separation required by 45 CFR § 164.504(f)(2)(iii) is supported by reasonable and appropriate security measures;*
- *Ensure that any agent, including a subcontractor, to whom it provides this information agrees to implement reasonable and appropriate security measures to protect the information;*
- *Report to the Plan any security incident of which it becomes aware;*
- *In accordance with 45 CFR §§ 164.502(e)(1)(ii) and 164.504(e)(2)(ii)(H), ensure that the reasonable and appropriate security measures are in place for any business associate it engages on behalf of the Plan; and*
- *Comply with the applicable requirements of Subpart C of 45 CFR Part 164 with respect to electronic Protected Health Information, to the extent that the Plan Sponsor carries out an obligation of the Plan under such Subpart."*

## Notes for Compliance Writer

**CRITICAL: Determine applicability first.** Many organizations incorrectly believe this applies to them.

### Applicability Decision Tree:

1. Does {{COMPANY}} sponsor a group health plan?
   - NO → Policy NOT applicable, remove from policy set
   - YES → Continue to #2

2. Is the plan fully-insured with <50 participants?
   - YES → Policy likely NOT applicable (insurance carrier is covered entity)
   - NO → Continue to #3

3. Is the plan self-insured OR large group (50+ participants)?
   - YES → Continue to #4
   - NO → Policy likely NOT applicable

4. Does the plan sponsor ({{COMPANY}}) receive ePHI from the health plan?
   - YES → Policy IS applicable, complete content
   - NO → Policy NOT applicable

### If Policy IS Applicable:

1. **Plan Document Amendment**: This is the most critical requirement - must amend legal plan documents
2. **Legal Counsel**: Requires attorney specializing in ERISA and employee benefits
3. **Separation**: Must create clear separation between benefits administration (ePHI access) and general HR (no ePHI access)
4. **Limited Class**: Designate specific individuals by title, not by name
5. **Coordination**: Close coordination with insurance carrier or TPA required
6. **Certification**: Some carriers require annual certification from plan sponsor

### If Policy is NOT Applicable:

Delete this policy from the policy set and document why it is not applicable in the HIPAA Security Management Program policy.
